slivar rules for CNVs: assets/cnv-slivar-functions.js
─────────────────────────────────────────

The authoritative description is the map at the top of assets/cnv-slivar-functions.js (every
step of cnv_moi(), plus the shared building blocks), and the two tables in it. What every named
scenario produces is in assets/moi_scenarios.md, generated by assets/cnv-slivar-moi-harness.js.
This file is only an overview.

SLIVAR_EXPR (conf/modules.config) calls two classifiers:

  --family-expr  <tag>:cnv_moi(fam, variant) == "<tag>"          one MoI tag per family and record
  --sample-expr  po_*:cn_po_origin(sample, variant) == "<origin>" parent of origin, per sample

cnv_moi(fam, variant)
├─ modellable?           chrM, a chrX/chrY record straddling a PAR boundary, unknown sex on
│                        non-PAR X/Y, a female on non-PAR Y -> no tag
├─ unknown_cn?           an affected sample, or a parent of an affected carrier, has no CN
├─ carrier children      affected, at least one parent in the family, CN != expected CN
├─ TRIO_TABLE            per carrier child: (kid, mom, dad tokens, origin) -> child call
│                        e.g. de_novo, inherited_maternal, recessive, possible_de_novo
├─ FAMILY_TABLE          all child calls + who is affected -> one family tag
│                        e.g. dominant (an affected parent transmits), inherited_maternal
│                        (an unaffected mother transmits), parental_mosaicism_candidate
└─ no row fits           mendelian_inconsistent if some CN can't come from the parents,
                         inheritance_unresolved otherwise

cn_po_origin(sample, variant)       maternal / paternal / both / ambiguous, or nothing
└─ get_transmission_origin()        also the TRIO_TABLE origin column, so MoI and PO always agree
   └─ get_parent_transmission()     what each parent can pass: one of two haplotypes (CN 0-3), or on
                                    non-PAR X/Y a father's single copy (X to a daughter, Y to a son);
                                    a father's X to a son and a mother's Y count as nothing

Expected CN: 2 on autosomes and in the GRCh38 PARs; on non-PAR chrX 1 for males and 2 for
females; on non-PAR chrY 1 for males. Every rule works on the deviation from it. A record that
crosses a PAR boundary has no single expected CN for a male, so it isn't modelled, for anyone.
