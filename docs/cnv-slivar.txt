slivar rules for CNVs: assets/cnv-slivar-functions.js
─────────────────────────────────────────

The authoritative description is the map at the top of assets/cnv-slivar-functions.js (every
step of cnv_moi(), plus the shared building blocks), and the table in it. What every named
scenario produces is in assets/moi_scenarios.md, generated by assets/cnv-slivar-moi-harness.js.
This file is only an overview.

SLIVAR_EXPR (conf/modules.config) calls two classifiers. Both decide per child; the mode of
inheritance is wired like the SNV pipeline, as one --family-expr per tag:

  --family-expr  <tag>:has_moi_call(fam, variant, "<tag>")        true if any child of the family
                                                                 gets that call from cnv_moi();
                                                                 valued with the affected members
  --sample-expr  po_*:cn_po_origin(sample, variant) == "<origin>" parent of origin, valued with
                                                                 the children it resolves

cnv_moi(sample, variant)
├─ affected child?       unaffected, or no parent in the family (a founder) -> no tag
├─ modellable?           chrM, a chrX/chrY record straddling a PAR boundary, unknown sex on
│                        non-PAR X/Y, a daughter on non-PAR Y -> no tag
├─ unknown_cn?           the child, or (for a carrier) a parent whose copies matter, has no CN
├─ carrier?              CN == expected CN -> no tag
└─ TRIO_TABLE            (kid, mom, dad tokens, origin) -> the call, e.g. de_novo,
                         inherited_maternal, recessive, possible_de_novo; when no specific row
                         fits: mendelian_inconsistent if the child's CN can't come from the
                         parents, inheritance_unresolved otherwise
   └─ on non-PAR chrX    the SNV pipeline's X-linked name, by the child's sex (X_LINKED_CALLS):
                         son: de_novo -> x_linked_recessive_de_novo,
                              inherited_maternal -> x_linked_recessive
                         daughter: de_novo -> x_linked_dominant_de_novo,
                                   recessive -> x_linked_recessive
                         (a daughter's single inherited copy keeps inherited_*)

The call looks at the child and its parents only. Who is affected elsewhere in the family (is
the transmitting parent affected, do sibs carry the CNV) is left to whoever reads the tags.
A parent who passes nothing (a father's X to a son, any mother's Y) is read as "normal", and
their CN isn't needed.

cn_po_origin(sample, variant)       maternal / paternal / both / ambiguous / denovo /
                                    possible_denovo, or nothing; for every child, affected or not
├─ get_transmission_origin()        also the TRIO_TABLE origin column, so MoI and PO always agree
│  └─ get_parent_transmission()     what each parent can pass: one of two haplotypes (CN 0-3), or on
│                                   non-PAR X/Y a father's single copy (X to a daughter, Y to a son);
│                                   a father's X to a son and a mother's Y count as nothing
└─ get_denovo_origin()              when no parent's copy explains the child: both parents known and
                                    normal -> denovo; one normal, the other unknown -> possible_denovo;
                                    a carrier parent (a Mendelian error) -> nothing

Expected CN: 2 on autosomes and in the GRCh38 PARs; on non-PAR chrX 1 for males and 2 for
females; on non-PAR chrY 1 for males. Every rule works on the deviation from it. A record that
crosses a PAR boundary has no single expected CN for a male, so it isn't modelled, for anyone.
